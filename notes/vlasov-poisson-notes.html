Notes on the Vlasov–Poisson System

2026-04-19 Plasma Physics Mathematical Physics

Background reading for the planned PIC solver. The Vlasov–Poisson system is the simplest kinetic model of a plasma: collisionless, electrostatic only, and still rich enough to show Landau damping, two-stream instability, and phase-space filamentation.

The equations

The state is a distribution function \(f(x, v, t)\) — a density on single-particle phase space — evolving under the collisionless Boltzmann (Vlasov) equation:

\[ \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + v\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + \frac{q}{m}E(x,t)\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial v} = 0, \]

the Vlasov equation: f is simply transported by the single-particle equations of motion

self-consistently coupled to the electric field sourced by the charge density it implies:

\[ \frac{\partial^2 \varphi}{\partial x^2} = -\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}, \qquad \rho(x,t) = q\!\int f(x,v,t)\,dv, \qquad E = -\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x}. \]

Poisson's equation closing the system

Read the Vlasov equation as a statement about phase-space flow: it's literally the continuity equation for an incompressible flow in \((x,v)\) generated by the single-particle Hamiltonian \(H_{1p} = \tfrac12 m v^2 + q\varphi(x)\). \(f\) is just carried along, unchanged in value, by that Hamiltonian flow — which is exactly why this system sits so naturally next to the Hamiltonian mechanics notes: Vlasov–Poisson is Liouville's theorem applied one level up, to a whole distribution instead of one trajectory.

From continuous f to particles: the PIC idea

A particle-in-cell (PIC) code never represents \(f\) on a grid in \((x,v)\) directly — that's too expensive in more than one or two dimensions. Instead it samples \(f\) with a finite number of macroparticles and cycles through four steps every time step:

The two interpolation steps (deposit and gather) are where most of the interesting numerical-methods questions live: which shape function you use to deposit a point charge onto a grid, and whether you use the same shape function to gather the field back, turns out to control momentum conservation. This is also where the planned project connects back to the structure-preserving theme — there are energy-conserving PIC schemes that trade the usual momentum-conserving formulation for one that respects a discrete energy invariant instead.

A minimal particle push

import numpy as np

def leapfrog_push(x, v, E_at, dt, qm=-1.0):
    """One leapfrog step for N particles.
    E_at(x) -> interpolated field at each particle position."""
    v = v + 0.5 * dt * qm * E_at(x)
    x = x + dt * v
    v = v + 0.5 * dt * qm * E_at(x)
    return x, v
