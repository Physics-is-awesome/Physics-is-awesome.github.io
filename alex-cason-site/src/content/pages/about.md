# Bio
I am an undergraduate physics student at the University of Tennessee, Knoxville, interested in the intersection of theoretical physics, geometric mechanics, plasma physics, and scientific computing. My current focuses is on developing mathematical and computational tools for studying complex physical systems, with particular interests in Hamiltonian dynamics, structure-preserving numerical methods, plasma physics, and general relativity.

My current projects span numerical relativity, gravitational-wave astrophysics, and geometric integration. I am developing a BSSN formulation solver for simulating Kerr black holes, investigating gravitational-wave memory in core-collapse supernovae for next-generation detectors, and contributing to the documentation of the Julia package GeometricIntegrators.jl. More broadly, I am interested in how geometric structure, conservation laws, and analytical mechanics can inform the design of reliable numerical methods for nonlinear physical systems.

I aim to bridge mathematical theory and computational implementation, from deriving governing equations from first principles to developing simulations that test and extend those ideas. I am particularly interested in the geometric foundations of continuum mechanics and kinetic theory, including the structure-preserving discretization of plasma and fluid systems.

Through research, independent software development, and technical communication, I seek to deepen my understanding of fundamental physics while contributing useful tools and ideas to the scientific computing community.
